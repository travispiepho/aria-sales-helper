# ARIA Knowledge Base — DISC Buyer Persona Framework
# Source: "Taking Flight!" by Merrick Rosenberg & Daniel Silvert
# Purpose: Real-time prospect identification and rep coaching during live sales calls

---

## OVERVIEW

DISC is a 4-style behavioral framework. During a sales call, ARIA listens to BOTH the rep and prospect, identifies the prospect's DISC style from verbal cues (words, pace, tone, intonation), and coaches the rep in real-time with suggested next steps tailored to that style.

**The four styles (bird metaphors used in training):**
- **D — Dominant (Eagle):** Direct, results-driven, fast-paced, bottom-line
- **I — Interactive (Parrot):** Enthusiastic, social, big-picture, spontaneous
- **S — Supportive (Dove):** Calm, steady, loyal, conflict-averse, harmony-seeking
- **C — Conscientious (Owl):** Detail-oriented, logical, methodical, quality-focused

---

## DISC STYLE PROFILES

---

### D — DOMINANT (Eagle)

**Core Traits:**
- Results-focused, big-picture vision, decisive
- Direct, assertive, competitive, confident
- Challenges status quo, takes risks for big rewards
- Self-starter, wants control of their destiny
- Impatient with fluff or excessive detail

**How They TALK (verbal/intonation cues for ARIA to detect):**
- Assertive, certain tone — even new ideas sound like expert opinions
- Speaks with authority and conviction
- Fast-paced, jumps right to the matter at hand
- Skips pleasantries, cuts to the chase
- Makes definitive statements (not questions)
- Interrupts or redirects if you give too much detail
- "Bottom line is..." / "What's the ROI?" / "Just tell me what I need to know"
- Commands more than asks

**Body Language (for rep to observe):**
- Stands tall, firm handshake, steady eye contact
- Exudes self-confidence

**What They Fear / Avoid:**
- Wasting time
- Loss of control
- Being taken advantage of
- Looking weak or incompetent

**How to SELL to a D:**
- Lead with the bottom line first — results, ROI, timeframe
- Be direct and confident — don't hedge or over-qualify
- Give them choices/options (they want control)
- Keep it brief — no fluff, no long stories
- Use competitive framing ("here's what sets us apart")
- Acknowledge their authority — let them feel in charge of the decision
- Don't oversell — state the facts, let them decide
- Respect their time: arrive prepared, move efficiently

**Suggested Next Steps when you identify a D:**
1. Immediately pivot to results: "Here's what we can do for you and by when"
2. Give 2-3 options at different price points — let them choose
3. Ask direct closing questions: "Does this work for you?"
4. Don't drag it out — if they're interested, close fast
5. Follow up with bullet-point summary (no essays)

**Red Flags (D disengaging):**
- Checking phone or watch
- Saying "just get to the point"
- Starting to wrap up the conversation
- Giving short, clipped answers
- Taking over the conversation to redirect

**Overuse warning:** If they get blunt/abrasive/domineering, they're stressed — back off and give them control

---

### I — INTERACTIVE (Parrot)

**Core Traits:**
- Enthusiastic, optimistic, people-oriented
- Lives in the moment, future-focused, big-picture
- Highly persuasive, naturally builds rapport
- Hates details and constraints
- Thirst for adventure, variety, stimulation
- Creative "out of the box" thinker

**How They TALK (verbal/intonation cues for ARIA to detect):**
- Big smile energy translates to voice — warm, upbeat, animated
- Loud, expressive, fills the room
- Laughs easily, tells stories, uses humor
- Exaggerates: "That would be AMAZING" / "That's the worst thing ever"
- Starts talking like they've known you for years within minutes
- Jumps between topics — hard to stay linear
- Uses "we," "together," "you're gonna love this"
- "I just have a feeling this is going to work out great!"
- Fast-paced but conversational, not aggressive like D
- Lots of enthusiasm even about small things

**Body Language (for rep to observe):**
- Big smile, wide eyes, hearty laugh
- Uses full body to emphasize points
- Takes up physical space, gestures broadly
- Initiates conversation with strangers easily

**What They Fear / Avoid:**
- Being ignored or excluded
- Losing social approval
- Boredom, repetition, constraints
- Rejection

**How to SELL to an I:**
- Build rapport FIRST — they buy from people they like
- Match their energy and enthusiasm
- Paint a vision — "Imagine how it'll look when..."
- Keep it exciting, use stories and examples
- Don't overwhelm with specs and details
- Get them to imagine the outcome/experience
- Use social proof: "Your neighbors/other homeowners loved..."
- Make them feel special and excited
- Move fast while the enthusiasm is high — I's lose momentum
- Ask for the sale conversationally, not formally

**Suggested Next Steps when you identify an I:**
1. Spend more time on relationship/rapport early
2. Use enthusiasm: "You're going to love how this looks"
3. Show portfolio/before-after photos — visual storytelling
4. Get verbal commitment while energy is high
5. Follow up fast — I's go cold quickly if you wait

**Red Flags (I disengaging):**
- Getting distracted, changing subject
- Phone checking, looking around
- Energy drops noticeably
- Short answers replacing their usual stories

**Overuse warning:** If they become disorganized, start talking their way around questions, or make promises they can't keep — they're in overuse mode

---

### S — SUPPORTIVE (Dove)

**Core Traits:**
- Calm, steady, patient, empathetic
- Seeks harmony, minimizes conflict
- Deeply loyal, builds lasting relationships
- Prefers familiar, predictable patterns
- Works behind the scenes, avoids spotlight
- Processes slowly and methodically

**How They TALK (verbal/intonation cues for ARIA to detect):**
- Soft, quiet, gentle tone — rarely raises voice
- Slow, deliberate pace
- Asks questions about others, listens carefully
- Warm and sincere, not performative like an I
- Says "we" and "the family" a lot
- Hesitates before committing: "I need to think about it" / "Let me talk to my spouse"
- Rarely says no directly — deflects or delays
- "I just want to make sure everyone's comfortable with this"
- Checks in frequently: "Does that make sense?" / "Is that okay?"

**Body Language (for rep to observe):**
- Soft smile, gentle demeanor
- Small gestures, doesn't take up much space
- Comfortable in one-on-one or small groups
- May seem quiet in first meeting — warms up slowly

**What They Fear / Avoid:**
- Conflict and confrontation
- Sudden change
- Making the wrong decision and letting others down
- Being pushed or rushed

**How to SELL to an S:**
- Go slow — don't rush them
- Build trust first — they need to feel safe before buying
- Acknowledge their hesitation as valid: "I totally understand wanting to take your time"
- Ask about the family, the home, what matters to them
- Reassure: past clients, warranties, your process, what to expect
- Don't pressure — it will kill the sale
- Show consistency and reliability — "Here's exactly what happens step by step"
- Bring in social proof from similar customers in similar situations
- Include all stakeholders — if spouse needs to be involved, involve them
- Make them feel looked after, not sold to

**Suggested Next Steps when you identify an S:**
1. Slow down, ask more personal questions about their home/family
2. Walk them through your process step by step — predictability calms them
3. If they hesitate, schedule a follow-up instead of pushing: "No rush — when would be a good time to reconnect?"
4. Offer to include spouse/family member in decision
5. Send a warm, personal follow-up (not a template blast)

**Red Flags (S disengaging):**
- Saying "I need to think about it" repeatedly
- Getting visibly uncomfortable or quiet
- Vague non-committal answers
- Mentioning they need to "talk to someone else" as a delay tactic

**Overuse warning:** If they become passive, overly deferential, or seem to be agreeing just to end the conversation — they're not sold, they're just avoiding conflict

---

### C — CONSCIENTIOUS (Owl)

**Core Traits:**
- Detail-oriented, logical, systematic, precise
- Focuses on accuracy and quality above all
- Makes decisions based on data, not emotion
- Independent, methodical, even-tempered
- Questions everything to ensure correctness

**How They TALK (verbal/intonation cues for ARIA to detect):**
- Flat, measured, unemotional tone
- Speaks slowly and precisely — words are chosen carefully
- Asks lots of detailed questions: "What's the exact square footage cost?" / "What primer do you use?"
- Wants specifics, not generalities
- Logical sequencing in speech
- "Walk me through your process" / "What certifications do you have?"
- Uses technical language if they know it
- Doesn't give off agreement cues — hard to read
- Will push back if something doesn't add up
- Patient listener — lets you finish before responding

**Body Language (for rep to observe):**
- Flat facial expressions, unemotional
- Strong eye contact but minimal nodding
- Arms at sides — not expressive
- Doesn't initiate physical contact
- Takes notes, writes things down

**What They Fear / Avoid:**
- Being wrong or making a poor decision
- Incomplete information
- Surprises or unexpected changes
- Emotional manipulation

**How to SELL to a C:**
- Come prepared with data, specs, references
- Be honest about limitations — they'll find out anyway
- Walk through your process in detail: "Here's exactly what we do, step by step"
- Answer every question fully — don't rush past them
- Provide written proposals, itemized quotes
- Don't pressure — give them time to analyze
- Use logic not emotion: "Here's why this product is the right choice based on..."
- Respect their intelligence — don't oversimplify
- Follow up with thorough written documentation

**Suggested Next Steps when you identify a C:**
1. Slow down and answer every question thoroughly
2. Offer a written proposal with full itemization
3. Share product specs, certifications, warranty details proactively
4. Set clear expectations: timeline, process, who's on the crew
5. Give them time — push too hard and they'll shut down; follow up with more data

**Red Flags (C disengaging):**
- More and more detailed questions (doubt is building, not curiosity)
- Asks for things in writing / says "send me something"
- Goes quiet after a question (something didn't add up)
- Mentions comparing other quotes

**Overuse warning:** If they seem stuck in analysis, can't decide, or keep finding new objections — they're in C overuse; offer to answer one more question then gently ask where they are in their decision

## REAL-TIME IDENTIFICATION FRAMEWORK

ARIA should listen for these signals and classify within the first 2-3 minutes of a prospect conversation:

### Quick Diagnostic Questions (ask mentally as you listen):
1. **Fast or even-paced speech?** → Fast = D or I | Even/slow = S or C
2. **Outgoing or reserved?** → Outgoing = D or I | Reserved = S or C
3. **Big-picture or detail-focused?** → Big-picture = D or I | Detail = S or C
4. **Definitive statements or lots of questions?** → Statements = D | Questions = C
5. **Warm/personal or logical/factual?** → Warm = I or S | Logical = D or C
6. **Competitive/ambitious tone or harmony-seeking?** → Competitive = D | Harmony = S

### Style Matrix:
```
              FAST-PACED
                  |
    D (Eagle) ----+---- I (Parrot)
    Task          |     People
    focused       |     focused
                  |
    C (Owl)  -----+---- S (Dove)
                  |
              EVEN-PACED
```

### Verbal Signals by Style:
| Signal | Most Likely Style |
|--------|------------------|
| "What's the bottom line?" | D |
| "How soon can you start?" (impatient) | D |
| Tells long stories, laughs easily | I |
| "You're going to love this idea..." | I |
| "I need to talk to my spouse" | S |
| "We've lived here 20 years..." | S |
| "What exactly is in the paint?" | C |
| "Can you send me something in writing?" | C |
| Short, clipped answers | D |
| Enthusiastic but jumps topics | I |
| Quiet, warm, takes their time | S |
| Measured, precise, unemotional | C |

## INTONATION SIGNALS FOR ARIA AUDIO ANALYSIS

Beyond words, ARIA should flag these speech pattern signals:

**D signals:**
- Pace: Fast, clipped
- Volume: Confident, projects authority
- Pauses: Short, purposeful
- Interruptions: Will redirect conversation
- Emphasis: On outcomes and numbers

**I signals:**
- Pace: Fast, variable, excitable
- Volume: Loud, expressive, rises with excitement
- Pauses: Rare — fills silence easily
- Laughter: Frequent, genuine
- Emphasis: On experiences and relationships

**S signals:**
- Pace: Slow to moderate, deliberate
- Volume: Quiet, never raises voice
- Pauses: Longer — processing, careful
- Tone: Warm, gentle, nurturing
- Emphasis: On family, home, comfort

**C signals:**
- Pace: Measured, precise
- Volume: Moderate, controlled
- Pauses: Strategic — thinking before speaking
- Tone: Flat, unemotional, professional
- Emphasis: On facts, process, logic

## COACHING PROMPTS FOR ARIA

When ARIA identifies a style, surface the following to the rep (displayed on screen during call, not spoken aloud):

### D Detected → Show Rep:
- "🦅 D-Style prospect — bottom line focus"
- "Lead with: timeframe + results"
- "Give them 2-3 options to choose from"
- "Don't over-explain — let them decide"
- "If they seem impatient, go to close NOW"

### I Detected → Show Rep:
- "🦜 I-Style prospect — relationship first"
- "Match their energy, use enthusiasm"
- "Show portfolio / before-after photos"
- "Paint the vision: 'Imagine how great this will look'"
- "Close while energy is high"

### S Detected → Show Rep:
- "🕊️ S-Style prospect — earn trust, go slow"
- "Ask about family, how long they've been here"
- "Walk through your process step by step"
- "Don't push — offer to include spouse"
- "Set a follow-up, don't force a decision today"

### C Detected → Show Rep:
- "🦉 C-Style prospect — data and details"
- "Answer every question fully"
- "Offer written proposal with full itemization"
- "Share certifications, warranties, product specs"
- "Give them time — follow up with more info"

## COMBINATION STYLES (Common Blends)

- **D/I:** Bold, charismatic, fast-moving. Wants results AND relationships. Lead with enthusiasm + bottom line.
- **D/C:** Demanding and precise. Wants results backed by data. Bring both efficiency and facts.
- **I/S:** Warm and enthusiastic but needs to feel safe. Build relationship, then reassure.
- **S/C:** Slow, careful, needs full info + time. Most risk-averse buyer — patience and data win.

## INTEGRATION NOTES FOR ARIA PHASE 3

1. **Transcript + intonation must BOTH be analyzed** — text alone misses D vs I (both fast) and S vs C (both careful). Intonation differentiates.
2. **Flag style within first 2-3 minutes** — enough signal to start coaching, refine as conversation continues
3. **Coaching overlay appears on rep's screen** — never spoken aloud during call
4. **Style can shift** — if prospect gets defensive, note it; S/C may go quieter, D may escalate, I may disengage
